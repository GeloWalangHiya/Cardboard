Section 01

What is the Purpose of Recycling Cardboard?

Cardboard is one of the most recoverable materials on Earth—recycling it turns everyday packaging back into valuable raw fiber.

Conserves Trees, Water & Energy

Manufacturing recycled cardboard requires 75% less energy and significantly less water than creating virgin cardboard from newly harvested wood pulp.

1 ton recycled saves ~17 mature trees

Reduces Landfill & Methane Emissions

When cardboard is buried in oxygen-starved landfills, it decomposes anaerobically and releases methane—a greenhouse gas far more potent than carbon dioxide.

Lowers municipal landfill volume

Powers a Circular Economy

Wood fibers in corrugated boxes can be broken down, pulped, and remanufactured into new boxes, paper bags, and egg cartons 5 to 7 times before fibers shorten.

Closed-loop sustainable supply chain
Section 02

Identify What Cardboard Is

Cardboard is a heavy-duty, thick paper-based material manufactured primarily from processed wood pulp (cellulose fibers) and recycled paper fibers. Unlike standard office paper, cardboard is engineered for rigidity, cushioning, and structural strength.

Recognizing the two main forms of cardboard helps you sort everyday household and shipping items accurately:

A

Corrugated Fiberboard (Shipping Boxes)

Made of three layers: an inside liner, an outside liner, and a wavy, arched middle layer called fluting. That fluted arch traps air, giving delivery and moving boxes superior crush resistance.

B

Paperboard / Chipboard (Cartons & Tubes)

A single, dense, flat layer of compressed paper pulp without wavy fluting. Commonly used for cereal boxes, tissue boxes, shoeboxes, snack cartons, and paper towel cores.

Section 03

Cardboard vs. Recyclable Cardboard

Not all cardboard can enter the recycling stream. Contamination from food grease, moisture, or plastic coatings ruins entire batches of paper pulp. Compare the key differences below:

Recyclable Cardboard

Clean, dry, uncoated wood-fiber products

Accepted
  • Clean Corrugated Boxes: E-commerce delivery boxes, moving boxes, and clean retail shipping cartons.
  • Dry Paperboard Packaging: Cereal boxes, pasta boxes, tissue boxes (remove plastic film window), and soda fridge packs.
  • Cardboard Cores & Tubes: Inner rolls from toilet paper, paper towels, and wrapping paper.
  • Clean Pizza Box Lids: If the bottom is greasy, tear off the clean top lid and recycle just the clean half!

Non-Recyclable / Contaminated

Soiled, wet, waxed, or plastic-lined items

Trash / Compost
  • Greasy or Food-Soiled Boxes: Oil-soaked pizza box bottoms and takeout containers—oil prevents paper fibers from bonding during pulping.
  • Wet, Soggy, or Moldy Cardboard: Water weakens fibers and encourages mold growth that contaminates clean recycling bales.
  • Wax-Coated Produce & Freezer Boxes: Shiny wax or heavy plastic coatings (e.g., frozen food boxes, waxy fruit crates) cannot separate in standard paper mills.
  • Foil-Lined or Glitter Packaging: Metallic laminated gift boxes and multi-layer foil drink pouches.

3 Golden Rules Before Placing Cardboard in the Bin

1. Empty & Strip Remove all packing peanuts, bubble wrap, foam inserts, and excessive plastic packing tape.
2. Flatten Completely Break down boxes flat to save up to 70% more space in collection bins and recycling trucks.
3. Keep Dry & Covered Store flattened cardboard indoors or keep bin lids closed so rain does not ruin the fibers.
Section 04

How Cardboard Recycling Helps the Community

Every flattened box placed in a recycling bin creates ripple effects that strengthen local economies, keep streets clean, and bring neighbors together.

Creates Local Green Jobs

Sorting centers, hauling logistics, and regional paper mills employ far more local workers per ton of material than landfill operations.

Cleaner Neighborhoods

Organized cardboard drop-offs and curbside collection prevent overflowing dumpsters, windblown street litter, and urban pest habitats.

Lowers Municipal Costs

Diverting bulky boxes from landfills reduces municipal tipping fees and generates revenue from recovered fiber sales that funds public services.

Shared Civic Pride

School recycling drives, barangay/neighborhood eco-hubs, and community reuse swaps foster environmental education and collective stewardship.
